This letter is issued by the Department of Computer Science and Engineering,
Presidency University, Dhaka, to provide formal institutional acknowledgement
of the above-referenced research study submitted for peer review.

1. NATURE OF RESEARCH

The above-named researcher (Rifat Miah) conducted a primary cross-sectional
observational survey study involving N = 601 enrolled university students
across 11 higher education institutions in Bangladesh. The study additionally
included semi-structured qualitative interviews with a purposive sub-sample
of N = 20 consenting adult university students. The research focused on
identifying academic burnout risk factors using supervised machine learning
and mixed-methods analysis.

2. ETHICS EXEMPTION BASIS

Upon review of the study protocol by the Department of Computer Science and
Engineering, Presidency University, it has been determined that this research:

   (a) Is observational and non-interventional in nature;
   (b) Involves no deception, no sensitive personal data beyond self-reported
       academic habits and psychological states, and no invasive procedures;
   (c) Involved only adult participants (aged 18 years and above) who provided
       voluntary, written, informed electronic consent;
   (d) Poses minimal risk to participants, consistent with normal educational
       survey research;
   (e) Was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the
       Declaration of Helsinki (World Medical Association, 2013).

On the basis of the above, this study is categorised as EXEMPT from full
Institutional Review Board (IRB) review under the criteria applicable to
low-risk educational and observational research.

3. INSTITUTIONAL AFFILIATION AND OVERSIGHT

Computer Science and Engineering, Presidency University, Dhaka. Presidency

## Alternative if Presidency University Cannot Issue

If getting a formal signed letter proves difficult, use this self-declaration format acceptable to many Q2 journals (PLOS ONE, BMC):

> *"The authors declare that this study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Formal IRB review was not required for this low-risk observational educational survey study. All participants were adult university students (18+ years) who provided voluntary, written, informed electronic consent prior to participation. The study did not involve any sensitive interventions, deception, or vulnerable populations beyond the university student context. The corresponding author (info@pu.edu.bd, Presidency University, Dhaka) accepts full responsibility for the ethical conduct of this research."*

This self-declaration is standard practice for many South Asian university research publications and is accepted by PLOS ONE, BMC Medical Education, Frontiers journals, and other Q2 publishers for minimal-risk observational studies.
